States of Matter Quiz Monday, October 26 · from Mme Seychelle's October 23 newsletter

Part one

The three states of matter

Everything in the whole world is made of tiny particles. What makes a solid, a liquid or a gas different is how those particles are arranged and how they move.

Solid

un solide

Packed tight in neat rows. They wiggle on the spot but never swap places.

  • Keeps its own shape
  • Keeps its own size
  • Rock, LEGO, ice, a spoon

Liquid

un liquide

Close together, but they slide and roll past each other.

  • Takes the shape of the cup
  • Keeps its own size
  • Milk, juice, honey, rain

Gas

un gaz

Far apart and zooming everywhere, bouncing off the walls.

  • Takes the shape of the room
  • Spreads out to fill the whole space
  • Air, steam, helium, your breath

The two-question trick

Stuck on one? Ask these.

1. Does it keep its own shape?

Yes → it is a solid. No → keep going.

2. Does it keep its own size?

Yes → it is a liquid. No, it spreads everywhere → it is a gas.

Pour a liquid into a different cup and you still have the same amount. A gas will not do that — it fills every last corner it can reach.

Practise by moving

Be the particles

You already did this in class. Do it again in the kitchen: someone calls out a state, you become the particles. Then swap, and you name the state they are acting out.

SOLID

Stand tall, feet planted, elbows touching. Wiggle all you like, but your feet stay put.

LIQUID

Stay close, shoulders brushing, and slide slowly around the room together.

GAS

Spread out as far as you can and zoom in every direction. Bounce off the walls.

Watch out

The tricky ones

Sand pours like a liquid, but every single grain is a tiny solid. Sand is a solid.

Honey is slow and gloopy, but it still takes the shape of the jar. Honey is a liquid.

Air is invisible, but it is still matter — it takes up space. Squeeze a full balloon and you can feel it.

A sponge is a solid with lots of little gas pockets hiding in the holes.

Part two · from class, October 16

Clay, leather and metal

These are natural materials. People take them from the earth, from plants or from animals, and change them into things we use every day.

CLAY — dug from the ground

Bricks · flower pots · bowls and mugs · roof tiles · bathroom tiles

LEATHER — from an animal hide

Shoes · belts · bags and purses · a baseball glove · a couch

METAL — mined as rock, then melted

Forks and spoons · cans · pipes · coins · a bike frame · keys

Sorting rule

Natural or manufactured?

Natural

Did it grow, or come out of the earth or an animal?

woodwoolcotton stoneleatherclay watersand

Manufactured

Did people have to make it by changing something else?

plasticglasspaper steela tirea brick nyloncardboard

Here is the clever bit: clay is natural, but a brick is manufactured — people dug up the clay, shaped it and baked it hard. Same with a tree (natural) and paper (manufactured).
